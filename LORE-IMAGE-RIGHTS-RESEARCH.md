## Source/rights conclusions

- The existing MLB and Hall source pages do provide relevant images in several cases, and exact image URLs can be extracted from the HTML. That establishes availability, not reuse permission. MLB's terms prohibit reproduction, distribution, display, and embedding without written permission except limited personal use. [MLB terms](http://mlb.com/official-information/terms-of-use)
- The Hall of Fame terms state that site content is copyrighted and that photographs displayed courtesy of third parties may not be downloaded or duplicated. The Hall's image-rights page directs users to request permission and warns that copyright, site-license, privacy, and publicity rights may apply. [Hall terms](https://baseballhall.org/footer/terms) [Hall image rights](https://baseballhall.org/discover-more/photo-archives/image-rights-reproduction)
- Wikimedia Commons is viable only file-by-file. Commons says files are public domain or under a Creative Commons license, but reusers must inspect the file page and provide the required attribution/license link. [Commons reuse guidance](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Commons:Reusing_content_outside_Wikimedia/licenses)
- LOC “No known restrictions on publication” and “Free to Use and Reuse” collections are the best candidates for historical players, but the implementation should preserve the exact LOC item URL, rights advisory, collection, creator, and image URL rather than relying on a search result or aggregator. [LOC Free to Use and Reuse](https://blogs.loc.gov/loc/category/free-to-use-and-reuse)
- The Smithsonian Tris Speaker record is not a safe unrestricted asset: the record exposes an image URL but also says “CC0 Usage Conditions Apply” and points to restrictions. Smithsonian terms say usage-conditions content is non-commercial absent permission. [Smithsonian object](https://americanhistory.si.edu/collections/object/nmah_1425073) [Smithsonian terms](https://www.si.edu/termsofuse)

## Safest product implementation

1. **Preferred production path: locally hosted, file-level-cleared assets.** For each image, store a provenance record beside the asset: source page, direct image URL, creator/collection, exact license or rights advisory, attribution text, retrieval date, and any restrictions. Recheck the source before release. This avoids fragile hotlinks and makes attribution auditable.
2. **Acceptable fallback: remote image only when expressly permitted.** A remote URL may be used only when the exact file license permits it (for example verified PD or CC BY/CC BY-SA) and the provider permits the intended embedding/serving pattern. Keep provenance metadata in the lore item and show a compact credit/link where required. Do not use MLB/Hall image CDN URLs as a rights workaround.
3. **Default UI fallback: no image.** If no exact image-level permission is documented, render the existing lore card without an image and retain a source link. This is safer than displaying an unlicensed portrait.

The practical first batch is Cy Young and Walter Johnson after exact LOC/Commons record verification. Tris Speaker's Smithsonian image should remain external-link-only unless the product is clearly non-commercial and the applicable restrictions are accepted. All current MLB/Hall-hosted images should remain deferred pending written permission or replacement with verified public-domain/Creative Commons assets.
